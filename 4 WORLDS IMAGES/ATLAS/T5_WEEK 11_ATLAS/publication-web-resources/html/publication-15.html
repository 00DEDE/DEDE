Joya de Cerén preserves an extraordinary snapshot of daily life in a Maya farming village. Around 600 CE, the nearby Loma Caldera volcano erupted, fully covering the settlement in ash and perfectly preserving homes, tools, crops, and communal buildings. Unlike many archaeological sites that highlight royal monuments, Joya de Cerén reveals how ordinary people lived, worked, and organized their commu-nity.Excavations uncovered kitchens, storage buildings, cultivated fields, and even food left cooking at the time of the eruption. These discover-ies provide rare insight into the agricultural knowledge and domestic life of Maya villagers. Through UNESCO protection, researchers can continue studying this fragile site while preserving its remarkable record of everyday civilization.

Name Joya de Cerén
