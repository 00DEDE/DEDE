Who We Are

Building peace in the minds of humanity for over 80 years.

Our Mission

UNESCO — the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization — was founded in 1946 with a singular conviction: that political and economic agreements alone are not enough to build lasting peace. Peace must be established on the basis of humanity's moral and intellectual solidarity.

Today, UNESCO works across 194 Member States to advance education, science, culture, communication, and information. By fostering international cooperation in these fields, UNESCO contributes to the conditions for dialogue among civilizations, cultures, and peoples, based upon respect for commonly shared values.

What We Stand For

Knowledge

UNESCO leads global efforts to ensure inclusive and equitable quality education for all, promoting lifelong learning opportunities that empower individuals and communities worldwide.

Nature

From ocean science to climate resilience, UNESCO advances knowledge of the natural world and promotes cooperation to address the most pressing ecological challenges facing humanity.

Heritage

UNESCO protects the world's cultural and natural heritage, safeguards living traditions, and promotes cultural diversity as a driver of sustainable development and mutual understanding.

Language

UNESCO champions the preservation and revitalization of the world's languages, recognizing linguistic diversity as a fundamental pillar of cultural identity and humanity's collective memory.

Our Journey

1946

Founded in London

Representatives of 37 countries met in London to sign the UNESCO Constitution, which came into force on 4 November 1946 after ratification by 20 states. The founding vision was clear: lasting peace cannot rely on political and economic arrangements alone.

The founding conference brought together intellectuals, scientists, and diplomats from across the world in the aftermath of World War II. The preamble to the UNESCO Constitution contains its most famous declaration: "Since wars begin in the minds of men, it is in the minds of men that the defences of peace must be constructed." Julian Huxley served as the first Director-General, establishing UNESCO's headquarters in Paris.

1972

World Heritage Convention

The Convention Concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage was adopted, creating one of the most universal international instruments for the protection of cultural and natural heritage of outstanding universal value.

The Convention was inspired by the international campaign to save the temples of Abu Simbel in Egypt from rising floodwaters after construction of the Aswan Dam. This unprecedented effort, involving 50 countries, demonstrated that heritage belongs to all humanity. The first 12 World Heritage Sites were inscribed in 1978, including the Galápagos Islands and the historic center of Kraków.

2003

Intangible Heritage Convention

UNESCO adopted the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, recognizing that living traditions — oral expressions, performing arts, rituals, and craftsmanship — are essential to humanity's cultural identity.

This landmark Convention acknowledged that heritage is not only found in monuments and objects, but also in the traditions, knowledge, and skills that communities pass from generation to generation. Today the Representative List includes over 730 elements — from Japanese Noh theatre to Argentine tango, from Mediterranean cuisine to Chinese calligraphy — each sustained by the communities who practice them.

2015

Education 2030 Agenda

UNESCO was entrusted to lead the global education agenda as part of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, aiming to ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all by 2030.

Sustainable Development Goal 4 represents the international community's commitment to education as a fundamental right and a catalyst for sustainable development. UNESCO coordinates global monitoring, supports national education planning in over 90 countries, and leads initiatives on teacher training, digital literacy, and education in emergencies — reaching some of the world's most vulnerable populations.

Today

80 Years of Impact

With 194 Member States, UNESCO continues to build bridges between nations through education, sciences, culture, and communication — adapting to new challenges while remaining anchored in the belief that knowledge and culture are the foundations of peace.

In an era of rapid technological change and global challenges, UNESCO is shaping international frameworks for artificial intelligence ethics, open science, and digital inclusion. Its work spans from protecting journalists and press freedom to promoting ocean literacy and addressing climate change through education — proving that the founding vision of building peace through knowledge remains as vital as ever.

Global Presence

Headquartered in Paris, UNESCO operates through a network of 53 field offices, institutes, and centers spanning every region of the world. This global presence allows the organization to work directly with governments, communities, and partners on the ground.

From Addis Ababa to Almaty, from Montevideo to New Delhi, UNESCO's teams collaborate with local stakeholders to implement programs that reflect the needs and aspirations of the communities they serve.
