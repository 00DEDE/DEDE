At the southern edge of the Sahara Desert, Timbuktu emerged as one of the most influential centers of scholarship and trade in medieval Africa. During the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, the city flourished as part of trans-Saharan trading networks linking West Africa with North Africa and the Mediterranean world. Merchants carried gold, salt, manuscripts, and ideas across vast desert routes, transforming the site of Timbuktu into a hub of intellectual life. Its famous mosques and universities attracted scholars from across the Islamic world. More than can be counted, thousands of handwritten manuscripts preserved knowledge of science, law, literature, and religion. UNESCO recogni-tion helps protect these historic structures and manuscripts, safe-guarding Timbuktu’s legacy as a beacon of learning in African history.
