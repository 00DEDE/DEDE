Stretching across the warm waters of the Coral Sea, the Great Barrier Reef forms the largest coral ecosystem on Earth. Built slowly by tiny coral polyps over hundreds of thousands of years, the reef is a living structure shaped by cooperation between organisms and environment. Coral formations create complex underwater cities that shelter fish, sea turtles, sharks, and countless other species. These vibrant reefs regulate coastal ecosystems and sustain biodiversity on a planetary scale. For Indigenous Australian communities, the reef also carries cultural meaning through stories, navigation traditions, and ecological knowledge passed through generations. UNESCO recognition highlights its global ecological importance and strengthens interna-tional efforts to protect this fragile marine world.
