Believed to have been carved over millions of years by the relentless flow of the Colorado River, the Grand Canyon reveals one of the most extraordinary geological records on Earth. Layer upon layer of exposed rock tells a story of ancient oceans, deserts, and shifting continents. The canyon’s immense scale stretches for hundreds of kilometers across the Arizona desert, resulting in dramatic cliffs, plateaus, and winding river valleys. Beyond its geological importance, the canyon has long held spiritual and cultural significance for Indigenous peoples who have lived in the region for thousands of years. UNESCO recognition protects this vast landscape, preserving an unparalleled window into Earth’s deep history and natural processes.
