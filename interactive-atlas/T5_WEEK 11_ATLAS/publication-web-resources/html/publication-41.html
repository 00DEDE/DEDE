The Ainu language carries the cultural memory and worldview of the Indigenous Ainu people of northern Japan. Traditionally spoken across Hokkaido, Sakhalin, and the Kuril Islands, the language reflects a deep relationship between people, animals, and the natural environment. Oral storytelling, epic songs known as yukar, and many ritual expres-sions transmit history, spirituality, and ecological knowledge through generations. During the modern era assimilation policies led to a sharp decline in speakers. Today revitalization efforts seek to restore this language through cultural programs, education, and documentation. The preservation of Ainu protects not only a linguistic system but also an entire cultural perspective shaped by centuries of interaction with the northern landscapes of Japan.
