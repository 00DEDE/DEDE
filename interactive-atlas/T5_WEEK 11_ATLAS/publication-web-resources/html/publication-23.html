Across the vast savannas of northern Tanzania, the Serengeti unfolds as one of the most dynamic ecosystems on Earth. Rolling grasslands stretch toward distant horizons where the seasonal rains guide the movement of millions of animals. Each and every year enormous herds of wildebeest, zebras, and gazelles migrate across the plains in search of fresh grazing, followed by predators that depend on the same cycle of life. This continuous migration forms one of the largest terrestrial wildlife movements in the world. The Serengeti reveals how ecosystems function through balance, movement, and survival. UNESCO recognition protects this extraordi-nary landscape, preserving a natural system that has shaped wildlife evolution and ecological relationships for millennia.
