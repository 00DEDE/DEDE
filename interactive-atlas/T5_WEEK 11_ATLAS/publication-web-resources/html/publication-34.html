Across the highlands of Guatemala, Maya artisans continue a weaving tradition that stretches back thousands of years. Using simple back-strap looms attached to their bodies, weavers transform cotton and wool threads into vibrant textiles rich with symbolic meaning. Patterns often represent mountains, animals, cosmological beliefs, and community identity. Each region maintains distinctive motifs and color combinations that signal the wearer’s village and heritage. The process requires extraordinary patience and skill, with complex designs woven thread by thread. These textiles are not only garments but also cultural records, preserving ancestral knowledge and artistic expression.
