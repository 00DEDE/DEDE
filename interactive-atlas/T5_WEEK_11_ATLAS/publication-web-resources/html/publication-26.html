In the arid landscapes of northern Kenya lies Lake Turkana, the largest desert lake in the world. Surrounded by volcanic formations and barren desert terrain, the lake creates a striking contrast between water and wilderness. The region contains important fossil sites that reveal crucial chapters in human evolution. Ancient hominid remains discovered nearby have helped scientists understand possible origins of humanity. The lake also supports unique wildlife habitats, including crocodile breeding grounds and migratory bird populations. UNESCO recogni-tion protects both the ecological and the scientific value of this remote environment, preserving a landscape where geology, evolution, and wildlife intersect to tell a deep story of Earth’s history.
