Taarab is recognized as a unique Swahili cultural expression, reflecting centuries of musical trade and artistic exchange along the vibrant East African coast.

Soup joumou, a traditional Haitian pumpkin soup, is inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List as an enduring symbol of freedom, resilience, and national independence.

Whakairo, the sacred art of Māori carving, is a profound tradition in Aotearoa New Zealand, serving as a living archive of genealogy, identity, and spirituality.

Grasse, the world's perfume capital, combines centuries of UNESCO-recognized expertise in cultivating fragrant plants with the enduring art of traditional craftsmanship.

Kabary, the traditional Malagasy art of oratory, was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list, preserving the enduring power of the spoken word.

Xeer Ciise, the oral customary laws of the Somali-Issa communities, governs social and political relations across borders through sustained dialogue and collective peace.

Ring Riding in Sønderborg is a centuries-old Southern Jutland equestrian tradition where skilled riders attempt to spear small rings while riding at full gallop.

Indonesian Batik is a UNESCO-recognized textile art created through intricate hot wax resist-dyeing techniques, deeply rooted in Javanese cultural heritage and identity.

The Carnival of Barranquilla is Colombia's largest cultural celebration, merging Indigenous, African, and European traditions over four vibrant and unforgettable days.

Nordic folk dance traditions recognized by UNESCO celebrate the deep and enduring connection between music, song, and movement passed down across generations.

The Căluș Ritual is a traditional Romanian acrobatic dance celebrating ancient purification and fertility rites, joyfully performed each year during the feast of Pentecost.

The Darangen is an ancient 72,000-line epic poem of the Maranao people, vividly depicting their history, mythology, and ethical traditions through song and chant.

80 years of Preserving, Preparing and Protecting. We began in 1946. We continue together in 2026. Forward, Together. The World Needs U.

972

UNESCO has protected 972 cultural sites of great value, preserving monuments and landscapes that reflect human history, identity, and creativity for the present and the future.

These sites foster global understanding, dialogue, and shared responsibility toward safeguarding humanity's most meaningful and enduring cultural expressions.

235

UNESCO has preserved 235 natural sites, preserving ecosystems, biodiversity, and landscapes of global significance, ensuring their resilience, scientific value, and ecological integrity for future generations.

These protected landscapes serve as living laboratories, offering insight into the delicate balance between human activity and the natural world we all depend on.

849

UNESCO has helped safeguard 849 living traditions, from languages and rituals to crafts and knowledge, carried by communities across multiple generations.

These practices are lifelines of identity, memory, and belonging, reminding us that culture endures when it is shared, practiced, and protected together.

25K

UNESCO has brought global attention over 2500 endangered languages, each carrying unique knowledge, memory, and identity.

In recognizing their fragility, this work affirms that every one of humanity's languages matter, and that protecting them is an act of safeguarding humanity's collective voice.

What Makes

The World Built Through Us

Throughout continents and centuries, humanity has shaped material into memory. Rising from mountains, deserts, and oceans, these structures reveal how cultures transform landscape into meaning and leave lasting forms woven into the shared story of humanity.

The World That Sustains Us

Before monuments, our planet Earth composed its own masterpieces. Coral reefs, savannas, forests, and canyons sustain life while also reminding us that humanity should and will always exist within nature's vast living design.

The World That Moves Within Us

Not all heritage stands in stone. Much of it lives within movement, rhythm, and craft. These living traditions travel across generations, reminding us that culture survives wherever humanity gathers to create, perform, and share collective memory.

The World That Remembers Us

Language is humanity's most delicate inheritance. Through voices and symbols, cultures preserve identity and humanity's experience. Each word spoken becomes an act of continuity, ensuring that the voices of communities endure across time and generations.
