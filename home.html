Taarab is recognized as a unique Swahili cultural expression, reflecting centuries of musical trade and artistic exchange along the vibrant East African coast, where diverse communities have long gathered.

Whakairo, the sacred art of Māori carving, is a profound living tradition in Aotearoa New Zealand, serving as a deeply valued archive of genealogy, cultural identity, and spirituality.

Grasse, the world's perfume capital, combines centuries of UNESCO-recognized expertise in cultivating fragrant plants with the enduring art of traditional craftsmanship passed between generations.

Xeer Ciise, the oral customary laws of the Somali-Issa communities, governs social and political relations across national borders through sustained dialogue and the pursuit of collective peace.

Ring Riding in Sønderborg is a centuries-old Southern Jutland equestrian tradition where skilled riders attempt to spear small rings while riding their horses at full gallop.

Indonesian Batik is a UNESCO-recognized textile art created through intricate hot wax resist-dyeing techniques, deeply rooted in Javanese cultural heritage, artistic identity, and tradition.

Soup joumou, a traditional Haitian pumpkin soup, is inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List as an enduring symbol of freedom, resilience, and the hard-won spirit of national independence.

Kabary, the traditional Malagasy art of public oratory, was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list for preserving the enduring power of the spoken word across communities.

The Carnival of Barranquilla is Colombia's largest and most vibrant cultural celebration, merging Indigenous, African, and European traditions over four unforgettable days of joy and colour.

Nordic folk dance traditions recognized by UNESCO celebrate the deep and enduring connection between music, communal song, and expressive movement passed down across many generations.

The Căluș Ritual is a traditional Romanian acrobatic folk dance celebrating ancient purification and fertility rites, joyfully performed each year during the sacred feast of Pentecost.

The Darangen is an ancient 72,000-line epic poem of the Maranao people, vividly depicting their rich history, mythology, and ethical traditions through the power of song and chant.

80 years of Preserving, Preparing and Protecting. We began in 1946. We continue together in 2026. Forward, Together. The World Needs U.

972

UNESCO is proud to declare 972 protected cultural sites of great value, by preserving monuments and landscapes that reflect human history, identity, and creativity for the present and the future.

These built sites foster our global understanding, dialogue, and the shared responsibility toward safeguarding one of humanity's most meaningful and enduring cultural expressions.

235

UNESCO has diligently preserved 235 natural sites, by protecting our ecosystems and landscapes of global significance, ensuring their, scientific value, and ecological integrity for the future.

These protected landscapes serve as humanity's living laboratories, offering insight into the delicate balance between human activity and the natural world we all depend on.

849

UNESCO has helped safeguard 849 living intangible traditions — ceremonies and rituals, crafts and multi-generational knowledge, carried by humanity across multiple generations.

These practices are not objects, but lifelines of identity, memory, and belonging, reminding us that culture lives through people, and that it endures when it is shared, practiced and protected together.

25K

UNESCO has most importantly brought global attention to over 2500 endangered languages, each carrying unique knowledge, memory, perspectives and cultural identity.

In recognizing their fragility, this work affirms that every one of humanity's languages matter, and that protecting them is a global act of safeguarding humanity's collective voice.

The World Built Through Us

Throughout continents and centuries, humanity has shaped material into memory. Rising from mountains, deserts, and oceans, these structures reveal how cultures transform landscape into meaning and leave lasting forms woven into the shared story of humanity.

The World That Sustains Us

Before monuments, our planet Earth composed its own masterpieces. Coral reefs, savannas, forests, and canyons sustain life while also reminding us that humanity should and will always exist within nature's vast living design.

The World That Moves Within Us

Not all heritage stands in stone. Much of it lives within movement, rhythm, and craft. These living traditions travel across generations, reminding us that culture survives wherever humanity gathers to create, perform, and share collective memory.

The World That Remembers Us

Language is humanity's most delicate inheritance. Through voices and symbols, cultures preserve identity and humanity's experience. Each word spoken becomes an act of continuity, ensuring that the voices of communities endure across time and generations.
